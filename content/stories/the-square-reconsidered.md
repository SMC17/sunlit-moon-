On a Tuesday in September, the Fountain Square file finally acquired a docket number.

That is a small civic event and a large one. For ten years the square has been a holding pattern: pavers, planters, a seasonal tree, the ghost of a fountain that once gave the place its name. Now a mixed-use proposal — 212 homes over rebuilt retail, a re-graded plaza, water restored to the axis of Church Street — sits before the [Evanston Plan Commission](/people/plan-commission). Status: **In review**.

The architect, [Elias Ward](/people/elias-ward), has a habit of talking about the ground before the sky. “Height is the argument everyone wants,” he said in a memo that has already been photocopied at [The Celery Stalk](/businesses/the-celery-stalk). “The question is whether the square is a room again.”

His drawings put a shallow basin on axis with Church, a grade that sheds toward Sherman, and a tower that steps back twice before it meets the skyline. It is quieter than the renderings that circulated in 2019. It is not quiet. Nothing that replaces a block in [downtown Evanston](/places/downtown-evanston) can be.

## A plaza that forgot it was a square

[Fountain Square](/places/fountain-square) is not a park. It is a paved civic room at Sherman and Church, historically marked by water, currently marked by the absence of a decision. Saturday still belongs to the [farmers market](/businesses/evanston-farmers-market). Weekdays belong to buses, students, and the particular downtown quiet of a city that faces a lake and a university and sometimes forgets the block in between.

Planning director [Maya Chen](/people/maya-chen) has spent three years asking the applicant to prove the plaza is not leftover. Her staff report, posted the Friday before the hearing, is almost literary in its stubbornness: *The ground plane shall be designed as a public room first, a development setback second.*

Rosa Delgado, who chairs the [Downtown Evanston Partnership](/businesses/downtown-partnership), has a simpler test. “Does the market still fit?” she asked, not rhetorically, at a Saturday preview. “If the fountain comes back and the peaches have to leave, we have made a postcard.”

## What Plan Commission will actually decide

The commission will not vote on peaches. It will vote on a planned development: bulk, shadow, the width of a through-block walk, the hours a water feature may run, the number of homes that count as affordable under a local formula that everyone in the room can recite and no one loves.

Alderman Nate Okonkwo has already said the interesting question is who the plaza is for at 9 p.m. on a January Tuesday. That is not a planning standard. It is, in Evanston, a kind of standard anyway.

None of this is settled. **In review** is the most honest status a city can give a drawing. The square has a name that promises water. The file now has a number. Tuesday night will do what Tuesday nights do: argue the next shape of a place in public, under fluorescent light, with a court reporter who has heard every version of *sense of place* already.

The fountain, if it returns, will have to earn the name again.
