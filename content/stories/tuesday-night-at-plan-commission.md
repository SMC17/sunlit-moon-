The [Evanston Plan Commission](/people/plan-commission) meets under lights that flatter no one, which is part of the honesty of the room.

Nine citizens. A staff table. A projector that has opinions about PDFs. Public comment counted in minutes that always run long. If you want to know what [downtown Evanston](/places/downtown-evanston) is about to become, this is the theater — not the renderings, the pauses.

Planning director [Maya Chen](/people/maya-chen) speaks in the particular English of a staff report: *findings*, *conditions*, *the ground plane*. She eats lunch, more days than not, at [The Celery Stalk](/businesses/the-celery-stalk), where packets have been marked in the margins for twenty years. The lunch counter is not on the docket. It is in the culture.

## How to watch

Watch who asks about shadow and who asks about 9 p.m. Alderman [Nate Okonkwo](/people/nate-okonkwo) tends toward the latter. Watch whether the applicant says “plaza” or “open space.” They are not the same noun. Watch the court reporter. When the room gets literary, she does not.

The [Fountain Square redevelopment](/developments/fountain-square-redevelopment) will have its night. So will the lofts on Main. The commission cannot make a square into a room by voting. It can refuse a leftover. That is not nothing.

## Why a place OS bothers with Tuesdays

Sunlit Moon indexes this room because a city is not only its lake light. It is the Tuesday when a drawing acquires a status — Proposed, In review, Approved — and a block acquires a future tense.

Fluorescent light, a basin on axis with Church Street, a market that still has to fit. The plot is slow. The stakes are the shape of the place.
