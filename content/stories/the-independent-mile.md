The [Main–Dempster Mile](/places/main-dempster-mile) is not a branding exercise, though it has been branded. It is a stretch of walk-up brick, a bookstore that behaves like a civic institution, a dining room that treats the sidewalk as an extra table, and an alley that already has opinions.

[Main Street Lofts](/developments/main-street-lofts) would convert two of those walk-ups into twelve work-live studios, storefronts retained. The application is **In review** at [Plan Commission](/people/plan-commission). Neighbors, as the file politely notes, are watching the alley.

## What independence looks like at street level

[Bookends & Beginnings](/businesses/bookends-and-beginnings) still hosts a calendar that doubles as a public square: readings, the quiet work of remaining open, the particular courage of ordering stock for a city that can drive to a warehouse. A few doors down, [Union Pizzeria](/businesses/union-pizzeria) shares the alley the lofts would use. Kitchens have 10 p.m. lives that floor plans do not always credit.

None of this is an argument against housing. It is an argument for housing that knows it is moving in with someone. Work-live, if it is honest, means the street keeps its daytime names.

## A mile is a system

Downtown has Fountain Square. Central Street has its own morning. Main–Dempster is the city’s most legible proof that a commercial street can still be a commons — not a theme, not a food hall with better lighting, a block where the people who sell books and fire and cardamom know who is arguing at Plan Commission.

The lofts may be a good idea. The alley will tell. Tuesday night, as usual, will be asked to decide a living arrangement in the language of bulk and setback, which is the language we have.
